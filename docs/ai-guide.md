# AI guide — Mac's Little Chefs Academy

**Written:** 2026-11-06 · **For:** Mac (shareable) and Jacob · **Companion:** [marketing-plan.md](marketing-plan.md)

The point of AI here is **getting Mac's evenings back.** He's the nutrition expert; the AI is a fast assistant that drafts, sorts and summarizes. He checks everything before it goes out.

Any good AI assistant works (Claude, ChatGPT, Gemini). One with web search is better for the research prompts. Copy the prompts below and fill in the brackets.

---

## Ground rules

- **Never paste kids' names, ages tied to names, photos of kids, or parents' contact details** into an AI tool. Use "Parent A / a 7-year-old" instead.
- **Mac checks every nutrition claim and recipe.** AI makes confident mistakes, especially with allergies and portions.
- **Check AI math** before using it for prices or budgets.
- **Rewrite in his own voice.** Parents can tell a canned post; edit until it sounds like him.

---

## 1. Save this once: the "about me" block

Paste this at the top of any prompt so the AI writes like Mac, without re-explaining every time.

```
About my business: Mac's Little Chefs Academy (a program of McKelvey's
Nutrition Services LLC). I teach hands-on cooking classes for kids in
[TOWN], Pennsylvania. I have a doctorate in nutrition. Kids cook real
food and learn why it matters. Ages [X–Y]. Classes are [length/format].
Website: macslittlechef.com.

My voice: warm, plain, a little funny, never preachy about food. No
medical claims. Short sentences. I'm talking to busy parents.
```

## 2. Time savers (weekly)

### Reply templates for signups

The site emails Mac each signup. Make the replies once, save them as Gmail templates, and send in one click.

```
[about me block]

Write 5 short email templates I can reuse to reply to parents who
signed up on my website:
1. New inquiry: thanks, next class times, price, how to book
2. Class is full: waitlist + next opening
3. Birthday party inquiry: package, price, what I need from them
4. School or group inquiry: ask about group size, dates, location
5. Follow-up if they haven't replied in a week
Leave [brackets] for things I fill in. Under 120 words each.
```

### A month of social posts in one hour

1. Pick 8–12 class photos (hands and food, no faces unless consent).
2. Describe each photo in one line (don't upload kids' faces to the AI).

```
[about me block]

Here are short descriptions of 10 class photos:
1. [kids' hands rolling turkey-veggie meatballs]
2. ...
Write an Instagram/Facebook caption for each: 1–3 sentences, one
kid-friendly nutrition fact, and a soft call to sign up at
macslittlechef.com. Vary the openings. No hashtag walls (3 max).
```

3. Schedule them all at once in **Meta Business Suite** (free).

### Take-home recipe cards (marketing that rides home in a backpack)

```
[about me block]

Write a take-home recipe card for [RECIPE] that [AGE] year-olds made
in class. Include: ingredients, 5–7 simple steps a kid can help with,
one "why this is good for you" line a kid would repeat at dinner, and
an allergy swap. Fit on a half page.
```

Jacob or a free Canva template adds the logo, the website and a **QR code to Mac's Google review link**.

## 3. Growth tasks (as needed)

### Find places to pitch

```
[about me block]

Search the web and list, within 20 miles of [TOWN], PA:
- township parks & rec departments, with their program-guide
  submission deadlines for spring and summer 2027
- elementary school PTAs/PTOs
- homeschool co-ops
- public libraries and YMCAs that run kids' programs
Give name, website, contact email/phone if public, and deadline.
Put it in a table.
```

Verify each contact before using it. Search results go stale.

### Pitch emails + one-page flyer

```
[about me block]

Write a short email to [PTA president / rec director] at [PLACE]
offering a [6-week after-school cooking class / one-time demo].
Mention: my nutrition doctorate, PA clearances, insurance, food-safety
certification, that kids actually cook, and price [$X per kid or
flat $Y]. End with one easy next step. Under 150 words.

Then write the text for a one-page flyer I can attach.
```

### Competitor and pricing check

```
Search the web for kids' cooking classes, camps and birthday parties
near [TOWN], PA. For each: name, price, class length, ages, format,
and what parents complain about in 1–3 star reviews. Then suggest
where I should price a [6-week class / 2-hour party / camp week] and
what I can offer that they don't.
```

Competitors already noted in [spec.md §6](spec.md#6-example-sites): Mini Chef Academy (Philly), Silly Spoons (Warrington), Ash Chase Culinary (Doylestown), Young Chefs Academy (Yardley).

### Lesson plans

```
[about me block]

Plan a [6]-week class for ages [X–Y], 60 minutes each. Each week:
one recipe kids can do mostly themselves, one nutrition idea in kid
language, a 5-minute game or demo, prep list, and allergy swaps.
Budget: about $[X] in ingredients per kid per class.
```

## 4. Monthly numbers check

Uses the spreadsheet in [marketing-plan.md §7](marketing-plan.md#7-what-to-track). Remove parent and kid names before pasting.

```
I run a kids' cooking class business. Below is this month's data:
inquiries (date, how they heard, booked Y/N) and classes (type,
kids, revenue, hours I spent including prep and driving).

[paste rows]

Tell me:
1. Which "how they heard" source brings the most BOOKED families
2. Revenue per hour of my time by class type
3. What to do more of and less of next month (max 3 each)
4. Anything that looks off or that I should double-check
Show the math.
```

## 5. Big decisions

Use these before spending money or time on something new.

**Argue against it:**

```
I'm thinking about [adding summer camp weeks / renting a kitchen /
hiring an assistant / raising prices to $X]. Here's my situation:
[numbers]. Give me the strongest case AGAINST doing it, what would
have to be true for it to work, and a cheap way to test it first.
```

**When can I drop a job?**

```
My kids' cooking business makes about $[X]/month from [N] classes.
Costs: ingredients $[X], supplies $[X], insurance $[X], mileage $[X].
Job I'd drop pays $[X]/month after tax and takes [N] hours/week.
How many classes per month would replace it? What's realistic given
[hours] free hours/week? Show the math and the risks.
```

**Parent feedback themes** (after a session, with a short survey or notes):

```
Here are anonymous parent comments from my last session:
[paste, names removed]
Group them into themes. What do parents love, what are they unsure
about, and what words do they use that I should put on my website?
```
