# Marketing plan — Mac's Little Chefs Academy

**Written:** 2026-11-06 · **For:** Mac (shareable) and Jacob · **Companion:** [ai-guide.md](ai-guide.md)

Mac teaches kids to cook real food, and he has a doctorate in nutrition. He works three jobs and just moved to Pennsylvania, so he doesn't know many people here yet. This plan is built around that: **find families in batches, not one at a time, and keep his weekly marketing time to about 2 hours.**

---

## 1. Positioning

Most kids' cooking classes around here sell *fun* (franchises, birthday-party kitchens). Mac sells **cooking taught by a nutrition doctor.**

One line to use everywhere (site, flyers, posts, emails):

> Kids learn to cook real food, and why it matters, from a nutrition doctor.

Who this speaks to most:

- Parents of picky eaters
- Kids with allergies or diet restrictions
- Young athletes and their parents
- Families trying to eat better on weeknights

**Careful:** describe classes as education, not treatment. No "cures," "fixes ADHD," or weight-loss promises. If Mac is a Registered Dietitian (RD), say so; it's a title parents and schools recognize.

---

## 2. Do these before pitching schools or towns

Schools, rec departments, libraries and camps will usually ask for these. Getting them first prevents stalled conversations, and showing them on the site builds trust with parents.

- [ ] **PA child clearances** for anyone working with kids: PA Child Abuse History, PA State Police criminal record check, FBI fingerprint check
- [ ] **General liability insurance**, with a certificate he can email on request
- [ ] **Food safety certificate** (e.g. ServSafe Food Handler)
- [ ] **Allergy policy**: what he asks families, what he won't cook with, what happens if there's a reaction
- [ ] **Photo consent line** in his signup or class paperwork ("OK to post photos of hands/food, no faces / faces OK / no photos")

Once done, the website gets a line like *"Clearances ✓ Insured ✓ Food-safety certified ✓"* (Jacob adds it).

## 3. Where to find families (ranked by return on his time)

| # | Channel | Why it fits | First step |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **School enrichment / after-school (PTA, PTO)** | One "yes" means a group of kids every week | Email 5 elementary PTAs near him with a one-page flyer ([ai-guide.md](ai-guide.md) has the prompt) |
| 2 | **Township parks & rec program guides** | Parents read these. Spring and summer guides fill early (Jan–Mar). | Call or email each nearby township rec office and ask how to be listed |
| 3 | **Homeschool co-ops** | Large PA community, need daytime enrichment | Find co-ops on Facebook and offer a trial class |
| 4 | **Libraries, YMCAs, scout troops, churches** | Free or cheap rooms with an audience already there | Offer a free 45-minute demo; collect signups |
| 5 | **Birthday parties** | Each party puts him in front of 10–15 families | Set a package price; Jacob adds a section to the site |
| 6 | **Local Facebook parent groups, Nextdoor** | Where PA parents ask "anyone know a kids' class?" | Join 3–5 town groups and answer those posts when they come up |
| 7 | **Google Business Profile** | Free, shows up for "kids cooking classes near me" | Create it, using a service area (hides home address) |
| 8 | **Farmers markets / community days** | Free demo table builds a signup list fast | Spring: ask the market manager about a vendor or demo slot |

**Not yet:** paid ads. Run them only once free channels show which message gets families to sign up, and only with a small test budget.

## 4. Reviews and referrals

These two cost nothing and matter most for a new name in town.

- **Ask every family for a Google review** right after a good class. Send the direct review link by text, and put it on a QR code on the take-home recipe card.
- **Use real reviews on the website** (first name + kid's age). The site currently shows samples.
- **Referral offer:** "Bring a friend, you both get $X off," plus a sibling discount.
- **Reply to every review**, good or bad, briefly and kindly.

## 6. The website's job

The site is a brochure with a signup form, and Jacob maintains it. Mac sends content by text or email.

**Done (2026-11-06)**

- "How did you hear about Mac?" dropdown on the signup form, so every inquiry says which channel worked
- Google structured data (LocalBusiness) so search can understand the business

**Needs Mac's content**

- Real class photos (6–10) and real reviews (4–6)
- **His town name.** "Pennsylvania" is too broad for local search. The town goes in the headline, page title and structured data.
- Short bio, ages, class length, price or "contact for pricing"
- Clearances/insurance badge line (after §2)
- **Birthday parties** and **For schools & groups** sections once he offers them

**Later, only if needed**

- Booking/payments (separate project and quote)
- A simple recipe page (good for search, only if Mac wants it)

## 7. What to track

One spreadsheet. A row per inquiry, and a row per class.

| Inquiries | Classes |
|---|---|
| Date | Date, where, type (school / rec / party / camp / private) |
| How they heard (from the form) | Kids enrolled |
| Booked? (Y/N) | Revenue |
| Notes | Hours spent, including prep, shopping and driving |

The number that matters is **dollars per hour of Mac's time, by channel and class type.** He only has so many evenings. Once a month, the AI prompt in [ai-guide.md §4](ai-guide.md#4-monthly-numbers-check) reads this sheet and says what to do more of.
